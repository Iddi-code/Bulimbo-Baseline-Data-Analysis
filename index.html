Bulimbo Mole Baseline Analysis

Automated analysis generated from the Kobo-labelled Excel export.

1. Executive summary

  • The baseline covers 161 farmers and 99 recorded variables.
  • Widespread infestation: 149 farmers (92.5%) reported current signs of mole infestation. This indicates that mole infestation is a widespread issue within the surveyed Bulimbo farmers rather than being limited to a small number of farms.
  • Field evidence: Active mole burrows or signs were observed on 134 farms (83.2%). This provides field-level evidence that the reported infestation is accompanied by observable mole activity.
  • Substantial infestation severity: 137 farmers (85.1%) reported infestation scores of 3–5. The distribution of severity should be considered when selecting farmers and locations for subsequent FRN testing.
  • Crop-loss concern: 59 farmers (36.6%) reported high or very high crop loss. This suggests that the FRN should document not only mole presence, but also the level and type of crop damage.
  • Existing farmer experimentation: 55 farmers (34.2%) reported that they had previously attempted to control moles. These existing practices provide an important starting point for documenting farmer knowledge and identifying practices that can be tested or refined through the FRN.
  • Training gap: 9 farmers (5.6%) reported receiving training on mole identification or control. This indicates limited exposure to formal mole management training among the surveyed farmers.
  • FRN implication: The baseline provides a reference point for moving from problem identification to farmer-led testing. Future monitoring should retain the core indicators of infestation, severity, crop damage, control practices and observed outcomes so that changes can be assessed consistently over time.

14. Programme interpretation

This report is designed to support the Bulimbo Farmer Research Network. The results should be used to identify the extent and distribution of the problem, understand existing farmer practices, and guide the next stage of farmer-led experimentation. Where the data show association rather than causation, the report deliberately avoids treating the relationship as proof of an intervention effect.
